## Intention
What this was ASKED to do, from the evidence — a resolved Jira ticket's summary (name the reporter and
ticket key), or failing that, the earliest commit's own subject/message if it states a purpose. If
NEITHER gives a real stated intention, say plainly that no original intent could be recovered from the

## What actually exists
How it works TODAY, grounded in the explore findings above — name the real files/functions/modules it
mentions. This is a description of current reality, not a design proposal and not a restatement of the
intention above.

## How they map
The actual comparison: does what exists TODAY (the section above) match the intention, or has it grown
past it, fallen short of it, or drifted into solving a different problem than originally stated? Be
concrete — name the specific gap or match, don't just say "mostly matches." If there was no recoverable

(condensed from the commit evidence — group related commits into a narrative, e.g. "introduced in X,
then Y reworked the retry logic, then Z fixed a race" — never mention a commit that isn't in the
evidence above).

## Problem areas — be careful here
State plainly what the evidence shows: which file(s) were touched most (the churn count), and which
commits looked like bugfixes (by subject). If the evidence lists NO bugfix-looking commits, say this
area has no visible bug history in the commits examined — do not manufacture a "this has had bugs"
narrative to fill the section. If a specific file/area churned unusually, name it and say concretely
why that's worth being careful around (a collision, a race, a repeated fix pattern — whatever the
subjects actually show) — this is the section someone about to modify this feature reads first.

## Summary
2-3 sentences: what this feature is, whether reality matches its original intention, and the single
thing worth knowing before touching it.

Tone: plain, evidence-first, no filler. A confident-sounding paragraph with nothing behind it is worse
than an honest "the evidence here is thin."
