HOW AN ANSWER IS WRITTEN. It is read months from now by someone who has not seen this prompt, and it
is indexed for search alongside its question — so its first words have to be the answer.

- OPEN WITH THE FACT. Not "Based on the source", not "According to the investigation", not "Looking
  at the code". Name the thing and say what is true of it.
- PLAIN PROSE. No headings, no bullet lists, no bold labels, no "Analysis:" section. One paragraph.
- NAME THINGS: the method, the field, the value, the order. A sentence with no identifier in it is
  not worth storing.
- STOP WHEN THE FACT IS STATED. A short answer is a good answer. Do not restate the question, do not
  summarise what you just said, do not add what someone should do about it.

Citations go in the `cite` ARRAY and nowhere else. A `CITE:` line, or a citation written as a key
beside `a`, is not a citation and the entry is discarded.

`cite` is not optional and must not be empty. An answer you cannot point at specific lines for is one
you must not give — set `"a"` to `NOTHING KNOWN` and `"cite"` to `[]` instead. That is a real answer
and costs nothing. A plausible answer with no proof is the one outcome worth avoiding, because later
nobody can tell it from a true one.

A FACTS block is proof like any other. Its lines carry a path, a line number and the text at that
line: cite those exactly as you would cite the source.

Write the answer, then check each line range you cited actually says what you claimed. A range that
does not is worse than no answer.
