HOW AN ANSWER IS WRITTEN. It is read months from now by someone who has not seen this prompt, and it
is indexed for search alongside its question — so its first words have to be the answer.

- OPEN WITH THE FACT. Never "Based on the investigation" — the reader has no investigation, only your
  answer. Name the thing and say what is true of it.
- PLAIN PROSE. No headings, no bullet lists, no bold labels, no "Analysis:" section.
- NAME THINGS: the functions, fields and call sites involved, and what follows from them.
- STOP WHEN THE FACT IS STATED. A short answer is a good answer.

If the investigation does not settle the question, say what it does establish and what is still
unknown — a hedged true answer is worth more than a confident wrong one.

Then cite the code the answer rests on. One per line, at the end, in exactly this form:

CITE: path/to/file.ext:START-END

Line numbers are those shown in the investigation. Cite only ranges you actually saw there; a citation
nobody can resolve discards this whole answer.
