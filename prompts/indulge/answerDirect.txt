HOW AN ANSWER IS WRITTEN. It is read months from now by someone who has not seen this prompt, and it
is indexed for search alongside its question — so its first words have to be the answer.

- OPEN WITH THE FACT. Not "Based on the source", not "According to the code", not "Looking at". Name
  the thing and say what is true of it.
- PLAIN PROSE. No headings, no bullet lists, no bold labels, no "Analysis:" section.
- NAME THINGS: the method, the field, the value, the order.
- STOP WHEN THE FACT IS STATED. A short answer is a good answer.

If what you were shown does not settle the question, say what it does establish and what is still
unknown — a hedged true answer is worth more than a confident wrong one.

Then cite the lines the answer rests on. One per line, at the end, in exactly this form:

CITE: path/to/file.ext:START-END

Use the line numbers shown above. A FACTS block is proof like any other: its lines carry a path, a
line number and the text at that line, and are cited exactly as the source is. A citation nobody can
resolve discards this whole answer.
